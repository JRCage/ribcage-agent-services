# CreditKeeper Customer Acquisition Sprint — 2026-10-26
Owner: Rowan. Purpose: first three legitimate paid audit orders, then recurring subscribers. Production intake/payment remain gated on QA and authorization.

## Product and buyer
Offer $49 pilot review (up to 25 documents), expected supplier-credit reconciliation with evidence; no guaranteed recovered funds. Ideal buyer: Texas landscape installation businesses with 20+ monthly supplier-return, warranty, or credit events; exclude mow-only. Secondary referral buyer: bookkeepers serving installers.

## Acquisition channels, ranked
1. Warm introductions from bookkeeping firms, vendors, contractors and relevant associations (ask permission; no referral fees without approval).
2. Individually researched outreach to qualifying installers via public business contacts, with documented opt-outs and CAN-SPAM compliance.
3. Demonstrations on synthetic records, shared in response to demonstrated interest.
4. Helpful, non-sales-first answers in landscaping and small-business communities, following posting rules.
5. SEO posts addressing exact questions: uncredited material returns; reconciling supplier statements; plant warranty credit tracking.
Do not pay for ads, buy bulk email lists, scrape personal data, or falsely imply an invoice error exists.

## First 14-day sprint
Day 1-2: repair QA blockers and get independent pass. Confirm safe public demo (no upload) and coordination contact.
Day 2-4: build 40-account lead list from company-owned public websites, association directories, supplier partner suggestions; dedupe; note installation activity and publicly listed business contact route.
Day 3-7: 10 tailored conversations/outreach attempts once compliance, identity, and contact path reviewed; seek 3 discovery talks. Keep intro concise and cite no unverified savings.
Day 8-14: expand to 20 additional qualified, individualized contacts; offer 10 synthetic demonstrations and aim for 2 explicit paid pilot commitments. Only start collecting confidential files and payment after secure intake, scope agreement and tested checkout.
Follow up once based on genuine interest; honor opt-outs promptly. Cold outreach is an experiment, not expected guaranteed conversion.

## Discovery script
'When materials are returned, how does someone verify the promised supplier credit reached your next statement? Does that happen often enough to be worth an independent review?'
If interested: 'We are testing a limited $49 review of up to 25 records after our secure intake is approved. The report distinguishes documented discrepancies from refunds actually received. Would seeing a fictional example be useful?'

## Funnel and weekly metrics
Lead -> verified ICP -> permissible contact -> actual reply -> problem-confirmed conversation -> synthetic demo -> audit offered -> paid order -> verified delivery -> subscription offer -> retained MRR. Store date, source, owner, consent/optout, channel, outcome, next action, expected credit volume. Count cash received, not invoices issued.

## Revenue gates
3 pilots x $49 = $147 one-time gross. At $99/month: 11 retained subscribers yields $1,089 MRR; 31 yields $3,069; 102 yields $10,098. Before subscription, measure recurring exception frequency and labor unit cost.
If no payments after 20 meaningful conversations, question the offer and market before doing more development. If document review produces false money claims, halt financial recommendations.

## Compliance and coordination
FTC CAN-SPAM applies to B2B commercial email too: accurate headers and subject, sender identity, valid postal address, simple opt-out and prompt honoring, no deception. Verify all requirements prior to outreach. Don't use unapproved sender address or offer. GitHub is canonical work log; for P1+ action/QA handoffs notify Johnny by email; customer data never in public repo. Ticket Recon remains separate.
