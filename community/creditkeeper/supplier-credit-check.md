# Did the material return reach your supplier statement?
A practical check for landscape installers and their bookkeepers.

A return receipt, a credit memo and a statement entry answer different questions. A return receipt alone does not prove that a credit was approved or applied. Check all three before claiming money is missing.

1. Keep the original invoice and the return receipt together.
2. Establish the accepted credit amount from the supplier's written approval, including any documented restocking charge.
3. Match the credit memo to that invoice and return. Do not match on amount alone.
4. Find the application on the supplier statement for the relevant period. A memo issued after the cutoff may belong to the next period.
5. List the difference and the evidence still needed. Ask for clarification before calling it an overcharge or recovery.

## Example evidence packet — fictional, not customer results
Scope: Example Supply, invoice INV-100, a single review period, USD.
This is a synthetic illustration. The three rows do not prove return acceptance or actual statement application.

| Source reference | Recorded event | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| INV-100 | Invoice | $240.00 |
| RETURN-1 | Expected return credit | $75.00 |
| CM-100 | Recorded posted credit | $25.00 |

Result: $50.00 unmatched expected credit, classified as **suspected_missing_credit**.
Confirmed recovery: none. Cash received: none.

Before supplier follow-up, verify the accepted return amount, statement cutoff, whether CM-100 was applied rather than merely issued, and whether another credit or restocking adjustment explains the difference. Do not add the invoice amount to the suspected missing-credit total.

Possible neutral question after evidence review:
“Could you confirm the disposition of RETURN-1 against INV-100? Our records show an expected $75 credit and CM-100 for $25. Is the balance pending, adjusted, or documented elsewhere?”

Balanced totals without the original invoice are still a missing-evidence case. Credits from another vendor must never offset this supplier's balance.
