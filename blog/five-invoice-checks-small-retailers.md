# Five Invoice Checks Small Retailers Can Run Before Paying a Vendor

*Published October 29, 2026 · Ribcage Agent Services · Small-business expense recovery*

An invoice can add up correctly and still contain an overcharge. Duplicate line items, unexpected quantities, changed rates, and missing credits are easier to catch when you compare the invoice with the records that explain it.

You do not need an expensive system to begin. A spreadsheet with a few consistent columns is enough for a first-pass exception report.

## 1. Look for duplicate invoice numbers

Group invoices by vendor, invoice number, and amount. If the same combination appears twice, flag it for review. **Do not automatically reject the second invoice:** vendors sometimes reissue corrected documents with the same number.

For a stronger match, include the invoice date, purchase-order reference, and whether either invoice has already been paid.

## 2. Compare billed quantities with what was received

A purchase order says what you intended to buy. A receiving record says what arrived. The invoice says what the vendor charged.

For each line, compare those three quantities. A billed quantity of 24 against a received quantity of 20 is an exception worth checking, but partial deliveries and backorders may explain it.

## 3. Check unit prices, not just totals

A line total of $240 might be mathematically correct for 20 units at $12, yet the agreed unit price may be $10. Keep a price-agreement column and flag rate changes that lack an approved explanation.

Pay attention to units of measure: one case, one pack, and one individual item are not interchangeable.

## 4. Reconcile returns and credits

A return receipt does not prove that the corresponding vendor credit was issued or applied. Track returns by vendor, item, quantity, return date, and expected credit amount. Then match each against credit memos and payment deductions.

A missing credit is a question for the vendor, not proof of wrongdoing.

## 5. Check tax, freight, and recurring fees separately

Compare shipping and service fees with the contract or purchase order. Verify whether taxes were calculated on the correct taxable items. Repeated small fees can be easy to miss when reviewing only invoice totals.

## A useful exception-report format

| Vendor | Invoice | Line | Expected | Billed | Difference | Reason to review |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Example Supply | INV-101 | Gloves | $40.00 | $48.00 | $8.00 | Unit rate differs |
| Example Supply | INV-102 | Shipping | $0.00 | $12.00 | $12.00 | Freight not on purchase order |

*All values above are fictional. A flagged difference is not a verified recoverable overcharge.*

## Protect the data while you check it

Start with fictional or redacted records. Remove customer names, card data, bank account numbers, personal addresses, and other unnecessary information before sharing a sample with a service provider. Preserve your original documents and require a human to approve any payment change or vendor dispute.
